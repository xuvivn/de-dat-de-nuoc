## 1. Mu Da Dan and Ba Ran

According to the strange records of the editorial board of the publication in **1988**, the image of **Da Dan** later in Muong folk also transformed into the character **Ba Ran** (*story of Mrs. Ran's Son*):

In the past, in **Phu Khe** commune (formerly called Cam Khe) there was a very deep lagoon with clear blue water called ***Mong lagoon***, at the end of the lagoon it opened into a deep, dark cave called ***Ba Dan cave***. In the cave, there are animals that specialize in catching passersby and eating them; If you cut off one head, another will grow; if you cut off one hand, another will grow. After asking an escaped girl to reveal that the elf was most afraid of **chicken shit**, the villagers wrapped chicken shit powder and applied it to their swords and spears, waiting in front of the cave entrance. When Mrs. Dan returned, everyone threw them at her face, causing her to scream horribly, stagger and then be cut into hundreds of pieces. The blood that accumulates on the ground turns into **mosquitoes, bedbugs, and centipedes**; flowing down and fading into **leeches and snakes**. Folk tradition says:

> *"Death to the Tiger family*
> *Breeds, mosquitoes, leeches, cannibals!"*

## 2. The motif of mortals marrying fairies

Besides, the motif of **a mortal getting married to a fairy** (*Cun Buom Bac – Cun Buom Bo* meeting *Nang A – Nang Hai* on the Tam river and Te river when Heaven's drum closes the gate) is also a mythical motif **worldwide**, similar to:

- the story *Marrying a Fairy Wife* of the **Ba Na** people,
- *Cringing with the fairy* of the **Ede** people,
- the story of *Sister Hang* of the **Thai** people,
- story *The Goat Shepherd and the Girls of the Sun* of **India**.

---

## 3. Why is this material important to tell?

- **Mother Da Dan** in the epic is a gentle mother who often asks for food but speaks with words of instruction. In folklore, she also has a dark side — a cannibal elf. These two aspects are not contradictory: the Muong people hold **both fertility and danger** in the same goddess image.
- When telling to children 9–10 years old, **there is no need to include the story of Mrs. Ran in Level 1**. You should only know to understand why Mrs. Da Dan is both warm and respectable.
- The motif "a man marries a fairy" helps the narrator realize: the story of two Cun boys meeting two fairies is **not an odd detail**, but one of the oldest motifs of humanity - the story of the sacred coming to live with the mortal, and then the mortal having to keep the sacred behind.

## 4. Suggestions for use in the telling

- **Before telling Chapter 6:** *"Muong people say that Lady Da Dan was born from the trunk of a rotten tree. But there are also other stories that say she was an old woman in a cave, both fertile and ferocious. Today we heard the story of her giving birth to humans."*
- **After telling:** you can ask the child: *"Why did the ancients put both the gentle and the scary in the same woman?"* — and there is no need to answer right away.
