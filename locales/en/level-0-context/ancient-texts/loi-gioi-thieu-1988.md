# Introduction 1988: *Mo Vai* And *Mo Leu*

> **Type of material:** Background material — introduction to performance
> **Use when:** preparing to tell Act I; Explain to listeners why this epic is sung at funerals
> **Source:** *Introduction: Mo sounds in the Muong night* and *Appendix A* of the consolidated version

---

## 1. Two large mo fields of the Muong people

During Muong funerals, the priest leads the deceased's soul through **two mo forests**:

- ***Mo lyc*** - a sacred poem that takes the soul beyond the mortal world. The soul must cross the Boi River estuary on the evil boat to Bui wharf, beg Mr. Tuan at Heaven's Gate to enter **Low Troi Muong** (through Hang Hao, Ma Cave, Theo Re Cave - the birthplace of humans and where the soul stands looking back at the whole world), then continue to **High Troi Muong** to meet the nine hunting gods, the fire god Ta Cam Cot, cross the Ly River through Liem La bridge to the pure Supermundane realm, get rid of all earthly debts to enter. immortal land.
- ***Mo leu*** (also known as *mo tieu* — mo tells stories) - is the epic ***Giving birth to land and water***. If in * mo lyc *, the soul is just a listener of divine stories, then in * mo leu*, the soul of the deceased becomes an **action character**: following every word of the mo's song, looking back at the time when the universe was still dark, the great flood that created the land, the great drought that gave birth to water, the giant sycamore tree falling and dividing it into one thousand nine hundred and nineteen villages, the Warg eggs hatching into clans, and the journey of building houses, finding fire, finding rice, making wine, casting bronze drums, building the dynasty. family, suppressing wild animals and eliminating evil spirits of the Lang family's ancestors.

In other words: ***mo lych* takes the soul away; *moan* for the soul to live again in the memory of the entire race.**

---

## 2. One night scene

When a member leaves this world to return to his ancestors, he must:

- spread a mat in the middle of the floor,
- next to the **nine-layer sticky rice tray** with five colors of blue, red, purple, yellow, white,
- in front of the **coffin made of hollowed out logs**,
- in one hand holding a **peacock feather fan**, in the other hand shaking a **small bronze bell**,
- in harmony with the orchestra of gongs, gongs, drums and trumpets.

A large funeral in the past performed the entire *Earth and Water* could last **for twelve consecutive nights**.

---

## 3. Rhythm *that* and rest

- ***That*** is a chapter, song, or paragraph in a performance.
- At the end of each *that*, he sings the inflection:

> *"That life is gone, that life has passed, I'll tell you that it's different."*

- Then he **strongly shook the bronze bell** to stop eating betel and drinking water.
- Padding at that break are bustling folk games: drum dancing, cheo sacrifices, cheo da leak, xuong singing - so that the grief subsides and the funeral night becomes a gathering night for the whole community before their ancestors.

---

## 4. Why is the name of the epic *Give birth to water*

The name *Give birth to earth* is just the name of **two opening chapters** (*Give birth to earth* and *Give birth to water*). But in the mind of the Vietnamese - Muong people, *"Land - Water"* is also **fatherland, race, source of survival**. Therefore, the names of those two sacred songs became **the general title for the entire epic**.

---

## 5. Notes for the young narrator

- There is no need to tell 9–10 year old children the entire funeral framework. Just **one opening sentence**: *"Once upon a time, when a person died, the priest sat in the middle of the house, shook the bronze bell, and told the whole village the story of the country that was born."*
- Retain the image **peacock feather fan, copper bell, nine-layer raspberry** — children remember images better than concepts.
- When you finish telling an episode, you can say: *"That life is gone, that life has passed, I'll tell you something else."* and then be silent for a beat - exactly the monk's resting rhythm.
