# Editorial Notes and Three Sources Merged

> **Material type:** Academic notes — text provenance
> **Use when:** adults need to know how this version was made; answer the question "why are there so many versions"
> **Source:** *Appendix C* of the consolidated version

## 1. Three unified sources

1. **2000 Edition** (*Compendium of Vietnamese Literature*, volume 41, Social Sciences Publishing House, 2000 - reprint of the 1988 edition introduced by Dang Van Lung, Vuong Anh, Hoang Anh Nhan): **26 *that***, the printed text of the book is concise, rich in music, accompanied by **33 blocks of academic notes**.
2. **1988 version (v1):** full text *Introduction 1988* on the performance of *mo fabric – mo leu*, Muong funeral and **6 *that* the head** has corrected many typographical errors in the 2000 edition.
3. **28-chapter version (v2):** The most complete text system includes all **28 chapters** - re-establishing the standard order in chapters XXV-XXVII, keeping the entire corresponding pair Introduction ↔ Laying the ground, the character Bui Rut, the story Thang Pap - Than Pam, chapters Looking for pigs to find chickens, Looking for buffaloes, and three complete parts of Chapter XXVIII.

## 2. Principles of literary production

- **No additional details beyond the three source versions.**
- Organized into **6 consecutive Acts, 28 Chapters**.
- Keep the poetic rhythm short (**4–7 words per line**), divide the stanzas according to scene and response.
- Miscellaneous modern prose and long mechanical repetitions in the oral version.
- Fully integrates valuable images and meaningful corrections from the 1988/2000 version:
  - *The moral of being a human being is not to steal*
  - *Valley soil soaks water*
  - *Goes to the wharf and swallows a leaf*
  - *Golden wings cover the sun*
  - *Four branches sink – thirty branches float*
  - *Ten colored talismans*
  - The countryside painting at the end of Chapter 28
- Remove redundant repetition due to recording errors in **Chapter 28 Part 1**; Move the *Revelations* and *Notes* section to the end of the book so that the epic flow flows seamlessly.

## 5. Why are there so many versions?

The three sources tell the **same story** but differ in some places, because:

- *Mo leu* is an **oral transmission** text — each mo, each muong can tell a different number of passages.
- 2000 edition **condensed** for inclusion in the book; The 28 chapter version keeps more **full oral transmissions**.
- Some characters and details (Ms. Bui Rut, Pap – Pam, Looking for Buffalo) only exist in a few lineages.
- Recording errors, image scanning errors, and punctuation errors over many reprints also create unintentional variations.

Therefore, this consolidated version **does not choose a single version**, but chooses the **most complete version** and clearly notes the differences in the Appendix.

---

## 6. Notes for the young narrator

- There is no need to tell children this part. This is **material for adults**.
- If a child asks *"why are there so many different versions of this story?"*, you can answer: *"Because this story has been told orally for many generations. Each man remembers a little bit, and the whole family keeps it together. It's like a song that everyone knows, but each person sings it in a different breath."*
- The most important thing when telling a story is **keeping the rhythm and the image**, not arguing about which version is right.
