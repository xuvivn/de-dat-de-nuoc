# Earth gives birth to water

*The epic of the Muong people — A consolidated version of the 1988/2000 edition (26 chapters), the 1988 reading version and the 28 chapter reading version*

## Introduction: Mo sound in the Muong night

In the ancient cultural treasure of the Vietnamese - Muong people, *Giving birth to the land* is not just a story told in poetry, but a sacred echo from the dawn of the race - where epics, myths, cosmic philosophies and Muong native memories blend together in the breathing rhythm of Muong land and sky.

According to folk performance tradition, when a member leaves this world to return to his ancestors, the mo (priest, keeper of the memories of the Muong) must spread a mat in the middle of the floor, next to a nine-tiered sticky rice tray of five colors, blue, red, purple, yellow, and white, in front of a coffin made of hollowed-out logs. Holding a peacock feather fan in one hand and shaking a small bronze bell in the other hand, along with a set of gongs, gongs, drums and trumpets, the shaman leads the soul through two large tombs:

- ***Mo fabric*** is a divine epic that takes souls beyond the mortal world. The soul must cross Boi River estuary on the evil boat to Bui wharf, beg Mr. Tuan at Heaven's Gate to enter *Low Troi Muong* (through Hang Hao, Ma Cave, Theo Re Cave - the birthplace of humans and where the soul stands looking back at the whole world), then continue to *High Troi Muong* to meet the nine hunting gods, the fire god Ta Cam Cot, cross the Ly River through Liem La bridge to the pure Supermundane world, get rid of all earthly debts to enter. immortal land.
- ***Mo leu*** (also known as *mo tieu* — mo tells stories) is the epic ***Give birth to land and country***. If in * mo lyc *, the soul is just a listener of divine stories, then in * mo leu*, the soul of the deceased becomes an action character: following every word of the mo's song, looking back at the time when the universe was still dark, the great flood that created the land, the great drought that gave birth to water, the giant sycamore tree that fell and divided it into one thousand nine hundred and nineteen villages, the Chieng eggs hatched to form clans, and the journey of building houses, finding fire, finding rice, making wine, casting bronze drums, building a royal court, suppressing wild beasts and eliminating evil spirits of the Lang family's ancestors.

A large funeral in the past, with a full performance of *Giving birth to water*, could last for twelve consecutive nights. At the end of each *that* (chapter, section), the mo's lyrics repeat the inflection - *"That life is gone, that is gone, I tell you something else"* - then the mo shakes the bronze bell to stop eating betel and drinking water. During that break, there are bustling folk games such as drum dancing, cheo sacrifices, cheo da leak, and xuong singing, so that the grief subsides and the funeral night becomes a night of gathering for the whole community before their ancestors.

The name *Give birth to land, give birth to water* is just the name of the two opening chapters (*Give birth to land* and *Give birth to water*), but in the minds of the Vietnamese - Muong people, *"Land - Water"* is also the homeland, the race, the source of survival; Therefore, the names of those two sacred songs became the common title for the entire epic.

This consolidated edition is crafted from three precious sources: the printed version of 26 *books* in the *Compendium of Vietnamese Literature* (volume 41, Social Sciences Publishing House, 2000 — reprint of the 1988 edition introduced by Dang Van Lung, Vuong Anh, Hoang Anh Nhan), the first 6 *books* reading with *Introduction 1988*, and the full reading of 28 chapters by the Muong people. The entire main epic text is reorganized into **6 Acts, 28 Chapters** continuously following the flow of 28 complete chapters, filtering the most concise and musical verses from printed books along with the corresponding layers, details and unique chapters of folk oral tradition (*finding pigs, finding chickens*, *looking for buffaloes*, *Ms. Bui Rut*, *worrying about dressing, finding palanquins*). Glossaries from Muong, place names and rare references are placed separately in the Appendix at the end of the book so that the sound of epic mo resounds seamlessly from the dark days of heaven and earth until the bright triumphant day of Dong Chi Tam Quan Ke Cho.

## APPENDIX A — Glossary of Muong Words, Characters and Places

### 1. Tu Muong and Cultural Concepts

| Words/Terms | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Mo leu / Mo pepper** | Ang mo tells the story of the history of heaven and earth, the race and the Lang family in chronological order (*Giving birth to the land*), distinguishing it from *mo fabric* (the epic of the gods taking the soul to heaven). |
| **That** | Chapters, songs, paragraphs in mo performance; At the end of each *that*, the priest often chants the transitional verse and then shakes the bronze bell to rest. |
| **Mr. Mo / Au Mo** | The shaman keeps the epic memories and presides over the ceremony at Muong funerals; *au mo* refers to a man who practices ghost worship and fortune telling. |
| **Mr. Dream** | The matchmaker who asks for a wife for the Lang family (in Chapter XVIII), is different from the *man* the shaman. |
| **Chu chapter** | The people and the Muong village community are under the authority of the Lang family (usually traveling in clusters *chu chu chuong nuoc*). |
| **Cun / Lang / Dao** | The ranks and leaders governed Muong in ancient Muong society (*Lang Cun* was at the top, below were *Dao*, *Chau*, *Va* who took care of military and administrative matters). |
| **Muong Mon** | Traditional self-name of the Muong people. |
| **Khau Lac (Khau)** | The bronze drum - a treasure symbolizing the sacred authority of the Lang family (*khau lac anh bronze*). |
| **Hot Chu, Khot Lui)** | Sacred objects, powerful treasures (such as magic jar fruit or *hot bag*, *hot lamp* in shaman's tools). |
| **Cardboard window / piece / wing / outside ** | The typical window system in ancient Muong stilt house architecture (*voong windows* are located in the main space connecting the worship and reception spaces). |
| **Xong** | Muong women's skirts have woven patterns at the top of the skirt (*deer skirt tip*). |
| **Break** | Duvet covers and pillowcases woven with traditional Lao and Thai patterns. |
| **Cotton** | Flowers, embroidered patterns (*cotton weaving*). |
| **The twilight child** | Sundew, an insect that runs on water. |
| **Cho / Basket / Nia / Dun** | Traditional measuring utensils: each *ch* is equivalent to about 5 kg of rice; *basket* ~0.70 m diameter holds rice; *nia* diameter ~ 1.10 m used to dry rice; The *dune* is about 3 m high and contains rice. |
| **Ang / Dap / Doi** | *Ang*: large pot, basin; *dip*: small woven salt container; *wait*: large bowl of soup. |
| **Chu tree / Thumb tree** | The sacred tree in ancient Muong consciousness symbolizes family lineage and community authority (*stone chu tree, dong chu leaf, brass flower, tin fruit*, related to the image of the mulberry tree). |
| **The Rope I'm Going** | A flexible forest rope used as a rope for plowing and harrowing. |
| **Explosion, explosion** | Ways to block streams to catch fish of the Muong people. |

### 2. Typical Characters, Gods and Things

- **Mr. Thu Tha - Mrs. Thu Thien:** Two primordial gods were born from two branches of a ninety-branched green tree after a great flood, opened the order of all things and ordered twelve months of the year.
- **Mr. Pong Peu:** The water god sits and weaves a fishing net, praying for heavy rain to create rivers and seas (*Giving water*).
- **Da Dan Lady:** The goddess reincarnated from the trunk of a rotten tree branch, gave birth to Silver Butterfly Cun and Bo Butterfly Cun, taught Tung Birds - Gaur Birds to weave silk to make nests, organized the incubation of Chieng eggs and later sewed brocade clothes for the king; In folk history, it is also associated with the story *The Snake's Son*.
- **Cun Butterfly Silver - Cun Butterfly Bo:** The two brothers got married to two fairies who were the children of the King of Heaven (*Nam A*, *Nang Hai*), forming two large lineages **Lang family** and **Dan family**, giving birth to ten children, the youngest being the pair **Thong Chim Tung male - Gaot female**.
- **Tao Chao Bird - Skylark:** The tiny pair of birds incubated *Po eggs and Chieng eggs* for nine months by using golden and silver wings to shade the eggs so that the eggs hatched into hundreds of nations and the Lang brothers.
- **Dit Dang, Lang Ta Cai, Lang Cun Can:** The first three brothers hatched from Chieng's eggs; The first two brothers could not overcome the evil spells that blocked their path, but **Lang Cun Can** bravely subdued the evil spirits, became the Lang who built houses, found fire, rice, wine, buffalos, cast bronze drums and was the father of the next Langs.
- **Va Hai Kip** *(also spelled Va Hai Chieng, Va Hai Kit)*: Lang Cun Can's younger sister; The illegal marriage between two brothers gave birth to twelve Demon boys, causing great disaster.
- **Ms. Bui Rut:** The brave hunter caught black and yellow turtles and learned how to build nine-compartment Muong stilt houses simulating the shape of turtle shells.
- **Visiting Cu Linh and Tun Mun:** *Visiting Cu Linh* is a scarab beetle that went to ask for fire from the fire god **Ta Cam Cot** but carelessly let the irrigation water turn off in the middle of the road so he was punished to live with dung; *Tun Mun* (along with Mun Mach, Mun Mong — horse flies) wisely peeked through the basket's slats and learned the trick of pulling the tinder to light a fire.
- **Lady Dat Cai Đình:** A skillful, kind-hearted woman of the Muong village (*good and wise child*), many times she took on the responsibility of going to heaven to meet **the Rice Fairy** to ask for rice seeds, to the house of **Mrs. Hung - Mrs. La** to ask for pig and chicken breeds, and to Khanh Hao land to learn the profession of growing mulberries, raising silkworms, and weaving brocade.
- **Pap boy - Pham boy and Mr. Lang Kham Dam** *(heterophone: La Khan Dan)*: Two buffalo herding boys got lost in Lang Kham Dam's family's fun festival and learned the secret to making wild leaf wine yeast.
- **Lang Cun Can's four wives and four children:** **Vam Dau Dat** *(son of King Binh Lac)* gave birth to **Cun Toi**; **The Water Head** *(son of King Vinh Vuong)* gave birth to **Cun Tang**; **A Sao, A Sang, A Rang** *(son of the Heavenly King)* gave birth to **Lang Cun Khuong**; and **Miss Ut** *(raised girl in the village)* gave birth to **Toong Ín**.
- **Tam Tach and his two children (Tam, Tach's son):** The kind and loyal servant found the spirit tree; was tricked into marrying by Lang Cun Khuong *cough chu* and then killed to use the bones to pull the tree. The two children took revenge and burned down the chu house. The blood flowed and turned into a **Giant Moon**.
- **Khan Dong - Miss Song Don:** The couple was forced by Lang to find the golden turtle; Thanks to the brindle dog to announce the news and his touching cry, **Buddha** saved him from the jaws of stone.

### 2. Strange investigation Chapter VI: Lady Da Dan, the story *The Son of Mrs. Ran* and the motif of people marrying fairies (1988 version)

According to the survey notes of the editorial board of the 1988 publication, the image of **Da Dan** later in Muong folk also transformed into the character **Ba Ran** (*the story of Mrs. Ran's Son*):

In the past, in Phu Khe commune (formerly called Cam Khe), there was a very deep lagoon with clear blue water called *Mong lagoon*, at the end of which it opened into a deep, dark cave called *Ba Dan cave*. In the cave, there are animals that specialize in catching passersby and eating them; If you cut off one head, another will grow; if you cut off one hand, another will grow. After asking an escaped girl to reveal that the goblin was most afraid of chicken shit, the villagers wrapped chicken shit powder and applied it to their swords and spears, waiting in front of the cave. When Mrs. Dan returned, everyone threw them at her face, causing her to scream horribly, stagger and then be cut into hundreds of pieces. The blood that accumulates on the ground turns into mosquitoes, bedbugs, and centipedes; flowing down and fading into leeches and snakes. Folk tradition says:

> *“Slay the Tiger family / Give birth to mosquitoes, leeches, and cannibals!”*
