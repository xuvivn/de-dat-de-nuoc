# Strange Investigation: Tich *Giving Wine Can* (2000 Version)

> **Type of material:** Variation — variation of Chapter 14
> **Used when:** tells Chapter 14 (Looking for wine); Compare the two versions for older audiences
> **Source:** *Appendix B.1* of the consolidated version

---

## Lead

In the printed version **26 that** (*Compendium of Vietnamese Literature*, volume 41, 2000), the story of making wine can **is not associated with Mr. Pap - Mr. Pam** in Muong Lang Cun Can, but tells about the exploits of **King Dit Dang** and the buffalo herdsman **Lo O - Lang Lieng**.

This is an important variation because it shows that the same motif "learning the secret of making yeast" can be attached to different characters in different performances.

---

## Original text

Tell stories and listen
Let's talk about and know:
Once upon a time, when King Dit Dang
Be the servant of the wealthy, be the lover of the poor
Debt with Cun Sam, Dao Soc, Cun Ngoc, Dao Khoang
They are all members of God's family.
King Dit Dang sent Khao Che and Khao Chot to collect the debt
Cun Sam and Dao Soc dismissed:
- "I don't owe you Dit Dang!"
Khao Che and Khao Chot got angry and ran down to catch the buffaloes and cows, holding the pan
Cun Sam and Dao Soc rushed out and chased away thirty blows, sending thirty more whips!

Khao Che, Khao Chot go fast one night, fast five nights
Seven nights, I returned to King Dit Dang.
King Diet Dang got angry and beat the gong three times
The soldiers filled the courtyard, filling three hundred mats.
The King commanded:
- "Whoever doesn't have rice, crushes rice in a jar
Those who don't have fish can drain the pond
If you don't have a sword, fight for a long time
If you don't have a knife, sharpen your knife to shine
A crossbow, three trumpets, a family of three hundred firebrands
Tomorrow, bring your troops to fight the enemy!"

Waking up in the morning, the king played three beats of the big drum and three beats of the small drum
When all the locusts and patches were gone, the soldiers picked flowers and sprinkled them on the road
Seven nights after arriving at Cun Sam, Dao Soc closed his station and let the paper runners inside.
Cun Sam and Dao Soc read the letter and went upstairs to ring the bell three times to call the soldiers
Arrayed troops on three hills to face battle.
Arrows shot in with a whooshing sound, arrows shot out with a thud
Blood flows like a ditch, the mouth fades!
The Dit Dang army won a big victory, Cun Sam - Dao Soc were defeated.

Bring your troops back to the intersection
Met her **Lo O, Lieng Lieng** herding buffaloes next to the mound
Dit Dang took them to herd cattle from the lower village to the upper village.
On Cun Sam day, Dao Soc makes a feast to worship the soul for three months
Lo O and Lieng Lieng attended and drank delicious wine
Going down to the fields to look for crabs and snails.
Crabs and snails teach the secret to making wine yeast:
- "Take the rice to dry in the sun and dry it
Go up the hill to get owl face roots, ebony tree skin, yeast vine, snakehead leaves, and royal grass
Go downhill to find dense grass, cypress grass, and black-legged, black-armed cypress trees
Pound it into flour and make cakes
Fold into baskets and incubate in straw
Three nights of smoking to get good yeast, healthy yeast!
Welcoming Mrs. Dit to work, Mrs. Danh came in to mix
Sprinkle yeast like salt or chili
Brew wine in a nest of washed leaves and pour in rainwater!

**Alcohol seeps in every round**
**Alcohol in soft drinks**
**Bitter wine is easy to swallow**
**Wine is as sweet as bee honey!**

Invite the king to drink one ladle, seven ladles of Dit Dang will make you drunk
Get up, grab your crossbow and shoot to kill!
Lo O and Lieng Lieng quickly ran down to the ravine to catch snails
At night, pound snails to relieve the king's alcohol.
From then on Dit Dai became a villager named Ke Cho:
Raising chickens has someone pounding the pieces
Duck herding has someone to fix it
Buffalo farming has poles and barns
The duck and geese have been taken over by a shepherd.

## Comparison notes

| | 28 chapter version (Chapter 14) | Version 2000 (examine this phenomenon) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Enamel Anthropology** | Pap - Pam, two buffalo herders | Lo O – Lieng Lieng, the buffalo herder was captured |
| **Instructor** | Lang Kham Dam in the happy crowd | Crabs and snails in the field |
| **Host Lang** | Lang Cun Can | King of Ease |
| **Air** | Light, happy, like a lucky lost trip | There is battle, there is drunkenness, there is detoxification with snails |

Both versions keep the **same core**: the secret to making yeast comes from very small, familiar things (forest leaves, tree roots, snails), and is given to those who know how to listen.
