# Level 3 — Standard Summary Outline (28 Core Chapters)

> **Intended use:** The only skeleton that adheres to all 28 Chapters of *Giving Earth to Earth* for ages 9–10. Each chapter includes the most condensed plot points to help the narrator quickly check the connection before entering Level 4.

## Act I: Opening Heaven and Earth and Origin of Race (7 Chapters)

### Chapter 1: Prologue — Telling an old story
- The invitation to listen to the story resonates in four directions: on the hill speaking to silver butterflies, in the water speaking to dusk kha, in the sky speaking to the Ngan River, in the homes of the elderly passing it on to children and grandchildren.
- The three stanzas "Wanting..." teach the way of life of the ancient Muong people: if you want silk, go to the hills to plant mulberries, if you want fish, go to the river and cast your nets, if you want to listen to stories, sit back.
- A picture of nothingness: there is no land on earth, there is no sky in the sky, there are no stars or paths yet; The land is still loose, the water is still loose.
- Everything "wants to wake up" but there is nothing to wake up; People don't have faces yet, brothers and sisters still fight over the religion of kings.
- **Highlight:** This chapter does not tell the events but creates a space for everything that follows to be born; The narrator's voice is the voice of an elderly person in the house.

### Chapter 2: Laying ground
- One year of heavy rain, water crossed seven U hills and flooded nine Bai hills; fifty days the water recedes, seventy days the water ebbs, leaving the first land behind.
- From the moist soil grows a green tree with ninety branches, branches reaching up to the sky, and in the canopy there are the voices of women and girls.
- The first two branches turned into Mr. Thu Tha and Mrs. Thu Thien, becoming a good couple, giving life to chickens with spurs, melons that can climb, bamboos that have thorns, and humans that can speak.
- The land is no longer fragmented, the water is no longer turbulent, the sky is no longer vague; In the water there are fish, crabs and shrimps, on land there are hills of Bai bamboo shoots, amaranth, pennywort.
- **Highlight:** Soil was formed after water, then from soil gave birth to trees, from trees gave birth to two ancestors; It's like opening each door, not like listing.

### Chapter 3: Giving birth to water
- Dry scene: roosters crow on the pass, evil cries on the mountains, the sun is shining but the harvest does not yield rice, the fishing nets produce no fish; The soil is fibrous, the cassava vines are withered, the cassava forest does not grow bamboo shoots.
- Nine months of drought, twelve years of sunshine: dried up streams, broken turtle shells, broken deer antlers, buffaloes eating frozen soil, people drinking dew water.
- Mr. Pong Peu sat weaving at the wing window, wishing for rain; The sky brought clouds, the Thunder God came down to scream, the Lightning goddess came down to scream.
- The first rain at night is the size of an eggplant seed, the morning rain is the size of a grapefruit, it rains nine nights and nine days; Four months the water recedes, seven months the water flows down, nine hills flow into one stream, nine streams flow into one river, nine rivers flow into one pool.
- **Highlight:** Water comes after thirst to save, not to punish; keep the rhythm of dry – wish – rain – recede and the relief at the end.

### Chapter 4: Giving birth to a sycamore tree
- The soil has loosened, the sycamore tree is growing brightly, as big as a tree: at night it's as big as a pestle, in the morning it's as big as a mat, and in the afternoon it's as big as a four-by-three stick.

- **Highlight:** The sycamore tree is the axis connecting heaven and earth; Its death is a reproductive death, not an accident.

### Chapter 5: Giving birth

## Act II: Establishing Lang, Building Muong and Creating Culture (9 Chapters)

## Chapter IV: The Cypress Tree Cycle — Finding, Cutting, Building and Burning (4 Chapters)
