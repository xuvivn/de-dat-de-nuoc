# Level 2 (Detailed Outline) — Act II: Establishing Lang, Building Muong and Creating Culture

> **Level goal:** Detailed outline of 9 chapters (8–16) of *Giving birth to land*, for storytellers 9–10 years old. This chapter tells about building a village, building a house, finding fire, water, rice, wine, pigs, chickens and buffaloes - each chapter is a step in perfecting Muong life.

---

### Chapter 8: Ease
- **Central Characters & Images:** Mr. Dit Dang, Chu Chuong Muong Nuoc, Evil Demon, Golden Dragon, Thuong Luong; In the landscape, there is land, heaven, and people, but there is no leader.
- **Detailed developments:**
 - Opens with the idea of "having" and "still missing": the earth exists, the sky exists, people have chosen a place to live, people have sought food and drink; But the Chu Chuong must be rich, the country must be rich, the leafy tree must have roots and branches.
 - At dawn, the villagers flocked to Dit Dang's house: walking on one side of the village, going around the mountains, the laughter echoed on Bai hill, the voice echoed on Gianh hill.
 - Dit Dang called his child at the door, ordered the mat to be spread, offered betel and areca nuts, and cooked a pot of green tea for the guests; Then he raised his voice and asked Muong what he had come for - was he called to hunt deer on Lai Lang hill, hunt deer on Ri Lay peak, or build a house?
 - Muong said: it's not hunting, it's not building houses; Since the beginning of heaven and earth, many people have sought food and shelter. Now, please welcome him to lead the army to keep the village safe and warm for the people.
 - Dit Dang didn't accept it right away: his stomach still wanted to eat fish, his stomach still wanted to eat rice and soup; He asked for offerings of ten large ducks, ten large animals to worship the ghosts in the village, nine burdens of gold, and twelve baskets of silver to worship the King of Earth and the King of Heaven.
 - Muong prepares the way: when it's sunny, we cut down trees to make way, when it rains, we lower branches and lower roots to make bridges; One receives in front, one receives in the back.
 - Three steps: Dit Dang goes down the first step, the cycle whispers; Down the middle step, the village was as silent as a sheet of paper; Going out into the yard and into the alley, everyone can see clearly.
 - As soon as you step onto the street, the wind rushes, dust swirls around the sky and earth, kumquat leaves and branches bend: a cursed ghost blocks the alley, sticks out ninety-nine red measuring tongues, shows ninety-nine tiger teeth; The yellow dragon bared its fangs, the blue-crested dragon bared its venom.
 - Dit Dang pulled out the silver sword and thrust the ivory-handled knife, but the evil dragon did not forgive him, and the cursed ghost did not abandon him; He had to turn around and go back, telling his mother to let him go home and not daring to take up arms again.
 - Muong took Dit Dang to the door, then dragged him to Lang Ta Cai's house; The conclusion promises the next story.
- **Meaning / Storytelling highlight:** This is the opening chapter of Act II, setting up the "three processions of lang" scene: Dit Dang, Lang Ta Cai, Lang Cun Can. The narrator maintains the solemn rhythm of Muong, but lets children see the hidden comedy: every man asks for food before accepting a job.
